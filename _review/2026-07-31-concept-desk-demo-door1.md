# Concept Desk demo — Door 1 assembly (idea-first)

> Source material for the Concept Desk demo page (Phase 2). Door 1 is told almost
> entirely from **real history**: the Commentary/RAID concept's two briefs, their genuine
> gate verdicts, the explore prototype, and the blind critique. **The only reconstructed
> element is the coaching dialogue** — the questions are brief-coach's standard moves,
> and every answer is taken from the stakeholder's own words in the real briefs. Each
> scene below is labelled `REAL` or `RECONSTRUCTED`.

## Why this example carries the demo

The real 2026-07-21 pilot accidentally produced a perfect controlled experiment:

- The **broad** brief went to the gate *without* coaching → **Needs work** (criteria
  2, 3, 4, 8 failed — four bets bundled, situation too wide, outcome unfalsifiable).
  One full review round spent learning what a coach surfaces in one question.
- The **narrowed** brief → **Ready**, all eight criteria pass.

So Door 1's pitch line is honest: *the desk front-loads, into a 30-minute
conversation, the narrowing that otherwise costs you a failed review round.*

## The coaching replay (RECONSTRUCTED — answers sourced from the real briefs)

Six exchanges for the demo page's steppable chat. Questions are brief-coach's coaching
moves; answers are condensed from the stakeholder's real text.

**1 · Open in their words** *(move: start from their words)*
— *Coach:* "Tell me about the idea the way you'd tell a colleague."
— *Stakeholder:* "PMs can't confidently manage risks, issues, decisions and commentary
in CP — it's hidden behind a modal, free-text, hard to review. They fall back to
offline trackers. We want to fix RAID capture, review, bulk updates, and reuse in
status outputs." *(the four bets, verbatim territory of the broad brief)*

**2 · One testable bet** *(move: which single one would tell you the most?)*
— *Coach:* "That's four bets — capture, review, bulk, reuse. If only one could ship,
which would teach you the most?"
— *Stakeholder:* "Review. Status prep is where the pain bites hardest — that's where
the offline trackers get created."

**3 · One user, one moment** *(move: picture one real person)*
— *Coach:* "Picture one PM. Where are they, what are they doing?"
— *Stakeholder:* "The day before a status meeting, opening one initiative, needing the
current RAID position and what changed since last period."

**4 · Falsifiable outcome** *(move: what would you count or watch?)*
— *Coach:* "How would you see that it worked?"
— *Stakeholder:* "In status prep, the PM finds every item changed since last period —
and who changed it — in under a minute, without the modal or an offline tracker."

**5 · Scope, said out loud** *(move: what are you happy NOT to solve?)*
— *Coach:* "And what are you deliberately not solving this round?"
— *Stakeholder:* "No new fields, no capture changes, no bulk view, no Sigma reuse, no
data-model rebuild. Surface, filter, diff what CP already stores."

**6 · Feasibility honesty** *(move: what are you assuming exists?)*
— *Coach:* "What are you assuming the data already has?"
— *Stakeholder:* "That updates carry author and timestamp… actually, I'm not sure."
— *Coach:* "Then that's a flag for the team, not an answer — it goes in the pack as an
open question." *(it became the real criterion-7 CANNOT VERIFY, routed to the Context
Pack backlog — and later the critique's #1 kill-risk. The flag was load-bearing.)*
