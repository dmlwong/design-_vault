Orbit Design Brain · Pilot playbook

Running the concept-intake pilot with your team

A short, no-infrastructure test of one question: does the pipeline actually help a teammate turn a rough idea into a testable concept? Everything below runs in a browser and two Claude Projects — no accounts to provision, and nothing anyone types leaves their machine.

~20 min one-time setup 2–3 testers 2 working days gate turnaround 1 intake owner

The loop at a glance

Steps 01–03 are the tester's, self-serve. Steps 04–05 are the design team's. A brief only reaches Explore once the gate says it's ready.

The one rule

Keep the Coach and the Reviewer as two separate Claude Projects. The Coach helps write the brief; the Reviewer judges it blind. If one chat does both, the reviewer rubber-stamps what it just helped author — and the gate becomes theatre.

1

Set up the two Projects

once · owner · ~20 min

You provision this once. It's two Claude Projects and a link — nothing installed.

1.1
Create the “Orbit Brief Coach” Project
In Claude, New Project → name it Orbit Brief Coach → paste the contents of brief-coach-project.md into its custom instructions → save.
1.2
Create the “Orbit Brief Reviewer” Project
Same again: New Project → Orbit Brief Reviewer → paste brief-reviewer-project.md. Optionally add your platform profiles to its knowledge so it checks concepts against real context.
1.3
Confirm the intake link works
Open the site, click Concept Brief Intake, type a line, hit Download .md. If a file lands in your downloads, the tester path works.
1.4
Name the intake owner & the clock
One person runs the Reviewer and replies within two working days. It can be you. Without a named owner, briefs pile up and the pilot stalls.
2

Pick your pilot group

once · owner

Small and real beats big and hypothetical.

2.1
Choose 2–3 teammates with a real, current idea
Not a made-up test case — an actual thing they wish existed. That's what stresses the pipeline honestly.
2.2
Mix coached and cold
Have some use the Coach first and some fill the form cold. Comparing coached vs cold briefs is itself the most useful data you'll get this week.
3

Run the loop, per brief

repeat · tester + owner

Four steps and a fork. The lane tag shows who's driving each one.

TesterA
Talk it through with the Coach (optional, recommended)
Open Orbit Brief Coach and describe the idea. It asks questions and hands back a tight brief in the right shape. Skipping this is fine — it's a valid comparison.
TesterB
Capture it in the intake form
Fill the form (paste and refine the coached brief if you have one), then hit Download .md. Everything stays in the browser — nothing is sent anywhere.
TesterC
Send the brief to the intake owner
Drop the .md file in Slack or email to the named owner. Done — the tester waits for a verdict.
OwnerD
Run the gate
Paste the brief into Orbit Brief Reviewer → get a verdict (Ready / Needs work / Blocked). Record it in the brief's ## Gate log and reply to the tester. Then, per the verdict:
Needs work → back to the tester
They re-open the form, use Import to reload the brief, fix the gaps the reviewer named, regenerate, and resubmit. This is the normal first outcome, not a failure.
Ready → into Explore
The design team runs explore in Claude Code to build a throwaway concept and a blind critique. The brief keeps its Gate log as its record.
4

Capture what you learn

throughout · everyone

This is the actual point of the pilot. The verdicts matter less than where the process helped or fought you.

Did the Coach draw the good material out — or paper over gaps and put words in the tester's mouth?
Did the verdict feel right, and were the reviewer's rewrite suggestions specific enough to act on?
Did any real idea have no home in the form — no matching surface, no field for something that mattered?
Where did people get stuck — the hand-off, the resubmit loop, or waiting for the verdict?

Log one observation per issue in design-brain/lessons/INBOX.md (or a shared thread). Those entries become the backlog for tightening the specs.

5

Decide what's real

after ~1 week or ~5 briefs

Everything is draft on purpose. Promotion is an owner decision made after evidence, never before.

5.1
Promote the specs draft → stable — if they earned it
If the coach and gate reliably helped, move them to stable. If not, keep iterating; there's no deadline on the label.
5.2
Tighten where the specs fought the evidence
Edit the coach/reviewer specs, re-run tools/build_project_bundles.py, and re-paste the updated bundles into the Projects.
5.3
Note the biggest gap
Without an Efficio Context Pack, the reviewer can't truly judge feasibility — it says so honestly instead of guessing. If that limitation bites during the pilot, that's your next initiative.
